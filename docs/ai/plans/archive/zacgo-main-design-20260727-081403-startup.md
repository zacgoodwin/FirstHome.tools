## Problem Statement

Homeowners, first-time buyers especially, do not know what maintenance their
home requires or when it is due. Neglected equipment fails; small issues become
expensive repairs. Source spec: `initial spec.md` (voice-memo discovery notes,
2026-07-26).

## Demand Evidence

n=1: the founder, a recent first-home owner building this for himself.
Founder's own words on the core value: "there are a lot of tasks that you
/don't/ know you should be doing. The goal of the wizard and the app is
surface everything, the easy stuff (cleaning gutters) to the surprising
(cleaning dish washer filter)."

Beyond n=1: no named users, no behavioral evidence. Adopted validation bar
(from cross-model challenge): 10 unrelated first-time homeowners complete the
wizard and subscribe to the feed; at least 5 complete a scheduled task within
30 days unprompted. Until that bar is met, demand is conviction, not evidence.

## Status Quo

Nothing, mostly: a spreadsheet, a fridge note, panic when the sump pump dies.
Incumbents exist but are not the real competitor: HomeZada ($59-99/yr, depth),
Homer (absorbed Centriq's users, AI manuals), Dwellin (free, address-based
instant schedule), Oply (done-for-you, covered metros). Centriq shut down
Jan 2025 and permanently deleted user data. Category retention is poor
(~70% of apps deleted after one use).

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

Target: first-time homeowners with no maintenance experience (the founder is
the archetype; no named external user yet — closing that gap is the
assignment). Wedge: the "what am I forgetting?" moment. A short wizard that
surfaces unknown-unknown tasks live as you answer, then installs a living
schedule into the calendar you already use via a subscribed ICS feed. Value
survives app churn because the reminder channel is the user's own calendar.

## Constraints

- Stack fixed at bootstrap: TypeScript 5 / Node 22 / npm, SvelteKit 2
  (Svelte 5), Supabase. Web/PWA only.
- Reminders via subscribed ICS feed (RFC 5545); no push/email infra.
- Out of scope (STRATEGY.md): home value/finance, smart-home integration.
- Out of scope (spec): tutorials, contractor marketplace, household inventory.
- Maintenance advice carries liability risk if wrong; rules need sources.
- Solo founder-builder, AI-assisted dev; wall-clock budget is nights/weekends.

## Premises

1. Demand is unverified beyond n=1. Validation bar: 10 strangers finish
   wizard + subscribe; 5 complete a task within 30 days unprompted.
2. Real competitor is "nothing" (spreadsheet, panic). Free incumbents exist,
   so feature count cannot differentiate; time-to-value and calendar-native
   delivery can.
3. (Revised after founder pushback.) The wizard is the value mechanism —
   surfacing tasks the user did not know existed. The risk is
   time-to-first-surprise: the plan must build live on screen as questions
   are answered, never batch-reveal at the end.
4. Subscribed ICS feed is the wedge: no notification infrastructure, value
   survives app abandonment. In-app calendar is secondary.
5. Trust is a feature post-Centriq: full data export from day one.

## Cross-Model Perspective

Codex (gpt-5.6-sol, cold read, verbatim highlights):

- Steelman: "FirstHome.tools is a 'what am I forgetting?' system for new
  homeowners. A short wizard reveals unknown maintenance tasks immediately,
  then installs a living schedule into the calendar they already use,
  preserving history without requiring another app habit."
- Key insight: the founder quote about unknown tasks "is the product. The
  wizard should optimize for time-to-first-surprise, not profile
  completeness."
- Challenged premise 1: "Founder demand at n=1 is conviction, not verified
  demand." Falsification bar adopted (see Premises).
- 48-hour prototype: no accounts; anonymous homes + opaque calendar tokens;
  a branching wizard over 8 categories (HVAC, water heater, gutters,
  dishwasher, dryer vent, smoke alarms, sump pump, refrigerator — Codex
  said "8-question"; adopted here as 8-category, since branching means more
  than 8 questions); rules as TS/JSON;
  plan updates beside the wizard after every answer; "You may not know this"
  highlights; stable /calendar/[token].ics; one-click JSON export; analytics
  for wizard completion, first-surprise time, subscription rate.

Synthesis: adopted the no-accounts cut and the analytics triad. Rejected
dropping completion tracking entirely (service history is a core later
promise; keep the mark-complete control, skip history-editing UI).

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Anonymous Loop (minimal viable) — CHOSEN

No accounts. Anonymous home + opaque calendar token. 8-category branching
wizard (HVAC, water heater, gutters, dishwasher, dryer vent, smoke alarms,
sump pump, refrigerator) building the plan live on screen, rules as JSON
data, /calendar/[token].ics subscribed feed, mark-complete, one-click JSON
export, analytics on wizard completion / time-to-first-surprise /
subscription rate. Each home gets two distinct opaque tokens: an edit URL
(bookmark to return and mark tasks complete; losing it means redoing the
5-minute wizard) and a read-only ICS feed URL — the feed URL gets pasted
into calendar apps and must never grant edit access. Recruited testers enter via tagged links (?tester=NN,
stored on the home row) so the validation bar is attributable per person.
Effort S (human ~2 weeks / CC ~2-3 days). Risk low. Exists to pass or fail
the premise-1 bar with 10 strangers.

### Approach B: Full MVP per spec (ideal architecture)

Accounts + Google OAuth, household sharing, home profile, 11-category wizard,
asset records with service history, documents, month calendar + agenda, ICS.
Effort XL (human ~3-4 months / CC ~2-3 weeks). Risk high: months of build
before any premise-1 evidence; auth/sharing/docs are pure cost until someone
besides the founder uses it. Rejected for sequencing, not for content.

### Approach C: Wizard as the front door (creative/lateral)

Approach A built as the public marketing site itself: wizard IS the landing
page; finish → shareable "Your home has 31 tasks you didn't know about"
report card + one-click ICS subscribe; magic-link email only to save. Effort
M (human ~3-4 weeks / CC ~4-5 days). Deferred: bolt the report card onto A
after the validation bar passes; it is the distribution wedge for the
100-user goal.

## Recommended Approach

A now, C's report card in week two if validation holds. The startup pass's
only job is reaching the premise-1 bar cheaply; every A feature serves that
test. B's content is not wrong — it is the builder-pass plan — but building
it before evidence exists violates premises 1 and 2.

## Open Questions

- Authoritative sources for maintenance intervals (spec open question;
  blocks the rules dataset; owner @Zac).
- Climate/region adjustments to intervals: v1 ships national defaults with
  a per-task interval override; regionalization deferred.
- Recruiting channel for the 10 testers (see Assignment).
- Token security posture (opaque, unguessable, revocable — needs an eng
  decision at build time), covering both per-home tokens: the edit-URL
  token and the read-only /calendar/[token].ics feed token.
- Feed regeneration semantics on completion: RRULE recurrence vs
  regenerated discrete events, and how stale-event risk is handled given
  calendar clients poll subscribed feeds as rarely as every 24h+ (the spec
  itself flags stale/duplicate events as a risk). Eng decision at build
  time; leaning discrete regenerated events, in-app agenda as the source of
  truth for "due now".

## Success Criteria

Event definitions (all logged to a Supabase analytics table, one row per
event, written server-side):
- "wizard started" = first wizard question rendered.
- "wizard completed" = final wizard step submitted.
- "first surprise" = first render of a "You may not know this" task card.
- "subscribed" = first HTTP fetch of a home's ICS feed URL by a calendar
  client (per token).

Criteria:
- Wizard completion rate ≥ 60% of starts.
- Median time-to-first-surprise < 90 seconds from wizard start.
- Validation bar: 10 unrelated first-time homeowners (attributed via
  ?tester=NN links) complete wizard + subscribe; ≥ 5 complete a task within
  30 days unprompted.
- 90-day outcome: 100 active users = homes (anonymous or account-backed)
  with ≥ 1 active schedule. Note: HEALTH-METRICS.md currently says
  "signup + ≥1 active schedule"; the scaffold ticket must amend it to count
  anonymous homes, or Approach A can never satisfy the official metric.

stores. Distribution of the product itself is Approach C's report card,
activated post-validation.

## The Assignment

Before any code: message five recent first-time homeowners you know (or post
in one first-time-buyer community) and ask two questions: "what home
maintenance are you tracking, and how?" Collect 10 people who commit to
running the wizard when it is live. The build is 2-3 CC days; the testers are
the long pole, so start recruiting now.

## What I noticed about how you think

- You pushed back on premise 3 with a reason, not a preference: "there are a
  lot of tasks that you /don't/ know you should be doing." That reframe
  (wizard = discovery mechanism, not data entry) is the sharpest product
  thinking in the session, and Codex independently landed on the same point.
- You adopted the harder validation bar without flinching when Codex called
  your n=1 conviction, most founders defend the comfortable number.
- You asked for two plans to compare before committing. That is a cheap,
  unusual, and effective way to expose what the startup framing quietly
  deletes (records, documents, history) and what the builder framing quietly
  ignores (distribution).
