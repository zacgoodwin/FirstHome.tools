## Problem Statement

Zac owns a first home and does not want to learn its maintenance needs the
expensive way. The app is the binder the sellers never handed over: every
system identified, every recurring task scheduled into the family calendar,
every completed job remembered per asset. Source spec: `initial spec.md`.

## What Makes This Cool

- The wizard that surprises its own author: answer "dishwasher: yes" and
  learn the filter wants cleaning every 2 months. The founder's words:
  "surface everything, the easy stuff (cleaning gutters) to the surprising
  (cleaning dish washer filter)."
- Reminders appear in the calendar the household already lives in
  (subscribed ICS feed) — the app never begs to be opened.
- The service history compounds: in year three, "when did I last flush the
  water heater?" has an answer.
- First planned upgrade is the "whoa" demo: photograph the furnace data
  plate and the asset appears with make/model/serial filled in.

## Constraints

- Stack fixed at bootstrap: TypeScript 5 / Node 22 / npm, SvelteKit 2
  (Svelte 5), Supabase. Web/PWA only.
- Reminders via subscribed ICS feed; no push/email infra.
- LLM features route through local Claude Code only (rules/CODING.md);
  extraction features require an eval suite before trusting output.
- Out of scope: home value/finance, smart-home, tutorials, contractor
  marketplace, household inventory.
- Nights-and-weekends wall clock; AI-assisted dev compresses build time.

## Premises

1. Zac is the user; usefulness to his household is the bar. Demand beyond
   n=1 is upside, not a gate.
2. (Carried from startup pass.) The wizard is the value mechanism — it
   surfaces tasks the owner did not know existed, live on screen as
   questions are answered.
3. (Carried.) Subscribed ICS feed is the delivery channel; in-app calendar
   is a view, not the reminder system.
4. (Carried.) Full data export from day one — this is the family's home
   record; it must never be hostage to the app (Centriq deleted its users'
   data when it died).
5. Record depth pays rent only when it is read later: service history and
   asset records are core; speculative fields are not.

## Cross-Model Perspective

Codex (cold read, same session): steelmanned the product as a "what am I
forgetting?" system that "installs a living schedule into the calendar they
already use, preserving history without requiring another app habit," and
pushed time-to-first-surprise as the wizard's design metric. Its 48-hour cut
list (skip accounts, sharing, documents, month calendar) shaped Stage 1's
ordering; its "skip completion history" cut was rejected here because the
binder's compounding history is the point of the builder pass.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A': The Home Binder, staged (personal-complete) — CHOSEN

The spec minus growth machinery, shipped in stages that each pay rent
immediately (stages detailed in Next Steps). Effort L total
(human ~2 months / CC ~1-2 weeks). Risk low.

### Approach B': Daily Driver (minimal viable)

Wizard → plan → ICS feed → mark complete; assets are name + category only.
Effort S (human ~1-2 weeks / CC ~2-3 days). Rejected as the end state — no
binder payoff, thin history — but adopted as Stage 1 of A'.

### Approach C': The Magic Binder (10x)

A' plus document ingestion (data-plate photo → asset; inspection PDF →
prefilled assets), home Q&A over asset data, recall watch on serials.
Effort XL (human ~4+ months / CC ~3-4 weeks). Risk medium (extraction
quality needs evals). Deferred: its first slice (data-plate capture) is the
designated first upgrade after A' lands.

## Recommended Approach

A' staged: Stage 1 is B' (reminders in the family calendar within days),
Stage 2 adds binder depth (asset records, documents, history), Stage 3 adds
household sharing + auth polish, then C's data-plate capture as the first
magic upgrade. Every stage is independently useful; no stage waits on a
later one to justify itself.

## Open Questions

- Sourced maintenance intervals: 8 launch categories now, remaining spec
  categories at Stage 2 (national defaults + per-task override for v1).
- Replaced-asset semantics: spec says preserve the old record; data model
  needs an explicit supersedes link (Stage 2 decision).
- File storage limits for documents (Supabase storage quotas; Stage 2).
- Token security posture for /calendar/[token].ics (opaque, unguessable,
  revocable) — same open question as the startup pass; one eng decision
  covers both.
- Data-plate extraction mechanism (Magic upgrade): the deployed app cannot
  call local Claude Code, so extraction runs as a local batch job — Zac's
  machine polls a Supabase queue of uploaded photos, runs local Claude
  Code, writes results back. A hosted-API path would need an explicit
  exception to the local-LLM rule (rules/CODING.md) from Zac.

## Success Criteria

- Stage 1: the household calendar shows FirstHome.tools reminders and the
  first surprising task Zac did not know about; first real task completed
  and logged.
- Stage 2: every maintenance-relevant asset in the house has a record;
  every manual/warranty PDF is attached; JSON export produces a complete,
  readable home file.
- Stage 3: partner has an account and has completed or annotated a task.
- Standing: gate tests green on every commit; evals ship with the first
  extraction feature (Magic upgrade), per rules/TESTING.md.

## Distribution Plan

Personal deployment on the planned Vercel pipeline (auto-deploy on merge to
main once /setup-deploy runs after scaffold). No other distribution needed
for the builder pass; if strangers ever want in, the startup-pass doc owns
that path.

## Next Steps

1. Scaffold ticket: SvelteKit 2 + Supabase + gate wiring (repo already
   anticipates this).
2. Maintenance-rules dataset: 8 launch categories with sourced intervals
   (HVAC, water heater, gutters, dishwasher, dryer vent, smoke alarms,
   sump pump, refrigerator — same list as the startup pass; write once,
   both plans use it). The spec's remaining categories (electrical,
   ventilation/ductwork, yard/irrigation, solar, basement/crawl space)
   join the dataset in Stage 2. Rules are data (JSON/TS), never code.
3. Stage 1 (B' core): minimal home profile (dwelling type + traits that
   gate wizard branches), wizard with live plan build →
   /calendar/[token].ics feed → mark-complete → one-click JSON export
   (premise 4 demands export from day one). Feed semantics: completing a
   task rolls it to its next due date and the feed regenerates as discrete
   events; calendar clients poll subscribed feeds slowly (up to 24h+), so
   the app's task list (the mark-complete screen, which Stage 1 already
   requires) is the source of truth for "due now"; richer calendar/agenda
   views arrive in Stage 2. Ship when the first reminder lands in the
   family calendar.
4. Stage 2: asset records (make/model/serial/warranty/install date),
   add-asset-after-onboarding, one-off maintenance events, archive /
   replace-with-history / delete flows, documents attach + browse, service
   history view, in-app calendar + agenda views, remaining rule categories.
   Supabase email auth arrives here; the Stage 1 anonymous home is claimed
   by token (visit your home URL while signed in → home attaches to the
   account).
5. Stage 3: household sharing as shared equal access for both adults —
   per-role view/add/edit/complete permissions are YAGNI for a two-person
   household and wait until a third user type (guest, contractor) exists.
6. First magic upgrade: data-plate photo → prefilled asset via local Claude
   Code, with an eval suite scoring extraction accuracy on 20 labeled
   photos before it ships.

## What I noticed about how you think

- "I want this for me so no kill criteria" — you scoped the risk out of the
  project before it existed. A tool with a guaranteed user of one cannot
  fail, it can only under-deliver, and that framing kept every later
  decision honest.
- Your pushback quote ("tasks you /don't/ know you should be doing") is a
  personal pain report, not a market thesis — which is exactly why it is
  credible. You are building the thing you reached for and found missing.
- You chose A'-staged over the shinier Magic Binder without prompting;
  sequencing delight after utility is the discipline most side projects
  lack.
